I wanted a place on the internet that's actually mine — not a profile inside someone else's
platform, not a feed, just a plain page with my name on it that I control top to bottom.

The design brief I gave myself was simple: make it look like a professor's website. The kind
that's mostly text on an off-white background, loads instantly, and hasn't changed its layout
since it went up — because it never needed to. No dark mode toggle, no animations, nothing
clever. The content is the site.

Under the hood it's [Astro](https://astro.build) generating plain HTML, with each blog post a
Markdown file in a folder. The whole thing deploys automatically when I push to
[the repo](https://github.com/rohankannan/rohankannan.github.io).

What will show up here: notes on what I'm learning, updates on research and projects, and the
occasional reflection like [my goals for the fall](/blog/goals-for-next-semester/). If you want
to know what I'm up to at any given moment, the [now page](/now/) is the place to look — and if
you want to reach me, my email is at the bottom of every page.
